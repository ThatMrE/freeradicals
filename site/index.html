Post before you scroll.

Social feeds stay closed until you publish something. Writing a post buys you a timed window — and the longer the post, the longer the window. When the timer hits zero the feed closes, and the next one costs another post.

Where it applies

Ten networks, each one toggleable.

It isn't a blanket ban on the site. reddit.com is blocked; a thread you chose to open is not. YouTube's home page is blocked; a video you searched for plays, minus the recommendation rail. Your messages and your own profile stay reachable. The target is the infinite feed, not the network.

What counts as a post

Filler is refused

aaaaaaa… and asdf asdf asdf don't clear the bar, however long they are. Length is necessary, not sufficient.

Repeats are refused

The same text can't buy a second window. Changing the casing or the spacing doesn't make it new.

You can't bank time

Posting again mid-window doesn't extend it. Reloading doesn't help either — the window lives in storage, not in the tab.

Optional: prove you published

Turn on proof mode and the clock starts when your post actually goes live on the network, not when you press the button.

What deliberately isn't prevented: turning the extension off. It's a speed bump between the reflex and the feed, not a lock. A tool that fights its owner gets uninstalled by the end of the week.

Install it

Not on the Chrome Web Store yet, so it installs unpacked — which takes about a minute and means you can read every line of what you're running.

  1. Download the package for your browser and unzip it.
  2. Open chrome://extensions and turn on Developer mode.
  3. Click Load unpacked and pick the unzipped folder.
  4. Open a feed. It'll be waiting for you.
Chrome .zip Edge .zip Firefox .zip Checksums SHA-256

Firefox needs a different build (no MV3 service worker), which is why it's a separate package. Packages are byte-reproducible from the source, so the checksums are worth checking.

On mobile: an Android and iOS app is built but not released. Android puts the same block screen over a feed app; iOS uses Screen Time, because no third-party app may draw over another there. Progress is in the repository.

Privacy, briefly

  • No server. There is nothing to send anything to.
  • No account, no analytics, no telemetry.
  • Your writing stays in your browser, and the settings page will export or delete it.
  • It runs on ten named sites, listed one by one in the manifest — no wildcard access.
